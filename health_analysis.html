Employee Health Span — Data Analysis Report

A synthesis of available data signals on the health and wellbeing of Cisco’s 85,639-person global workforce, with insights, risk flags, and prioritized recommendations.

Analysis based on: Optum YTD 2025 / Pulse Feb 2026 · Wellthy T12 Mar 2026 · RethinkCare Feb 2026 APJC Well-being FY25 · MMB Q2 FY26 · Mexico Clinic Mar 2026 Author: Global Benefits Insights team · Dashboard build output

Executive Summary — 4 Key Findings

Finding 1 — Strength
EAP utilization is 58% above BoB benchmark (3.94% vs 2.5%). Employees who engage report 23% reduction in absent days and stress. The program is working where employees use it.
Finding 2 — Risk Signal
Q4 2025 EAP utilization hit an 8-quarter low (2.9%). While Q1 2026 has recovered, this downward trend needs active monitoring — it may signal stigma increase or access friction.
Finding 3 — Critical Gap
Korea is the only market globally with NO mental health insurance coverage. Japan/Taiwan/Korea also show severely low vacation utilization (28-36%) signaling structural burnout risk.
Finding 4 — Cross-Region Pattern
Financial stress (Taxation) is the #1 presenting EAP issue in both EMEA and LATAM. This is a systemic signal: mental health and financial health are deeply linked for a significant part of the global workforce.
1 EAP & Mental Health Program Effectiveness
★ Strength

EAP outcomes are genuinely strong — the data supports the program

The Optum EAP delivers measurable outcomes: employees post-engagement show a 23% reduction in absenteeism, 23% improvement in stress, and 22.9% improvement in presenteeism. Concretely, that means 1.8 fewer absent days and 2.3 more productive days per engaged employee following an EAP interaction.

At 3.94% Cisco-HC utilization (vs 2.5% BoB benchmark), Cisco is running 58% above the global benchmark. If the 1.8-day absenteeism reduction is applied to the 1,800 global EAP cases in 2025, that represents roughly 3,240 working days recovered in a single year — a tangible business outcome from a relatively small program cost.

CSAT of 94.4% and 95.1% timely access rates indicate the delivery experience is strong, removing "bad experience" as a barrier to reuse. The 22.6% vendor engagement (vs 17.3% BoB) shows employees engage with the broader LiveWell platform, not just crisis counseling.

3.94% util vs 2.5% BoB 94.4% CSAT 23% absenteeism reduction 1.8 fewer absent days/EE 22.6% engagement (vs 17.3% BoB)
⚠ Risk Signal

Q4 2025 utilization decline: seasonal or behavioral shift?

Quarterly annualized utilization fell from 4.6% (Q1 2025) to 2.9% (Q4 2025) — a 37% reduction within 12 months and the lowest of 8 trailing quarters. The YTD blended 3.8% remains above benchmark, but the Q4 individual quarter reading is concerning.

Three hypotheses merit investigation:

  • Seasonality: Q4 (Oct-Dec) is often quieter. Check prior years' Q4 patterns.
  • Program awareness fatigue: Initial launch enthusiasm may have faded. Quarterly comms might need a refresh.
  • Stigma increase: Macro factors (layoffs, uncertainty) sometimes paradoxically suppress help-seeking as employees fear being perceived as struggling. This is the most concerning hypothesis and requires qualitative follow-up.

Q1 2026 Pulse data (Jan 4.7%, Feb 4.5%) suggests a recovery, which supports a seasonality explanation. However, the recovery should be confirmed through Q2 before the trend is marked as resolved.

Q4 2025: 2.9% (8-qtr low) -37% QoQ decline within 2025 Jan 2026: 4.7% (recovery) Feb 2026: 4.5%
ℹ Data Quality

EMEA’s headline utilization is Portugal-driven — not broadly distributed

EMEA reports 6.42% EAP utilization, the highest of any region. However, Portugal alone accounts for >90% of all EMEA cases at 38.2% utilization (vs 4.2% Portugal BoB). This is almost certainly an onsite-clinic attribution effect, where clinic visits are coded as EAP cases.

Removing Portugal from the EMEA aggregate would likely put EMEA utilization at or below the 2.5% BoB benchmark. This matters because the EMEA headline number suggests a healthy demand signal that doesn’t exist uniformly across the region. Program managers designing EMEA-wide EAP interventions should use country-level breakdowns, not the regional aggregate.

Similarly, Norway (16.2%), Australia (11.6%), and Israel (7.0%) all run significantly above their country benchmarks in Pulse data — suggesting pockets of genuine strong utilization distinct from the Portugal attribution effect.

Portugal: 38.2% util (vs 4.2% BoB) Norway: 16.2% (vs 7.9% BoB) Australia: 11.6% (vs 2.9% BoB) EMEA ex-Portugal: likely at/below benchmark
⚠ Underperformance

Canada is the only region running below EAP benchmark

Canada Cisco-HC utilization is 1.63% vs the 2.5% BoB benchmark — a 0.87pp gap on a population of 2,512 employees. With only 41 EAP cases in 2025, a small change in case volume would significantly move the number. The low absolute case count means variance is high, but the persistent below-benchmark reading is worth acting on.

Importantly, Canada’s RethinkCare penetration is the highest of any region at 7.80%, demonstrating that Canadian employees are willing to engage with wellbeing programs. This rules out general engagement resistance and points instead to specific EAP awareness, access, or stigma issues. The top presenting issues (Anxious/Worry, Parenting) are consistent with the rest of the global population, so there is no obvious country-specific topic that might be driving employees elsewhere.

Recommendation: Joint Optum-Canada program review focused on referral pathways, manager awareness, and whether the Canada EWS (Employee Wellbeing Solution) variant has differentiated positioning from the US EAP.

1.63% util vs 2.5% BoB (-0.87pp) 7.80% RethinkCare (highest region) 41 cases YTD (small n) Top issue: Anxious/Worry (consistent globally)
2 Wellbeing Program Analysis (Wellthy & RethinkCare)
★ Growing

Wellthy engagement is growing but a key outcome metric has declined significantly

Wellthy engagement has grown from 6.1% (2025 annual) to 6.9% (trailing 12mo ending Mar 2026). Engaged lives increased from 3,170 to 4,209 (+33%). Active care projects grew from 611 to 816 (+34%). Hours saved for US employees grew from 18,162 to 20,248. By engagement metrics, the program is scaling successfully.

However, the Q1 2026 outcome survey raises a flag: "More present at work" fell from 100% (Q4, n=29) to 33% (Q1, n=53). Critical caveat: the Q4 result was based on only 29 respondents and is statistically fragile. A 100% result from n=29 cannot be assumed to represent the population. The Q1 result (33% from n=53) is still small but directionally more trustworthy.

The most reliable metric trend is NPS: 93 (Q4) → 81 (Q1). A 12-point NPS drop is meaningful even at these sample sizes and should trigger a structured QBR conversation with Wellthy. The "less stressed" metric also slipped from 93% to 83% (-10pp). The caregiving support outcomes need continuous monitoring as the program scales to a larger, potentially more complex case mix.

Engagement +33% YoY (3,170 → 4,209) Hours saved: 20,248 (US) NPS 93 → 81 (-12pts) "More present" 100% → 33% (small n) "Less stressed" 93% → 83% (-10pp)
★ Strong Momentum

RethinkCare shows strong early trajectory; Canada leads penetration

RethinkCare launched globally in August 2025 and has grown from 3,722 to 4,145 enrolled employees by February 2026 (+11.4% in 7 months). This represents 4.84% of Cisco’s total global headcount as a cumulative reach figure, which is strong for a relatively new program.

Canada leads all regions at 7.80% penetration (196/2,512 EE), followed by LATAM (5.61%), US (5.52%), EMEA (4.30%), and APJC (4.28%). The inverse relationship between Canada’s EAP underperformance and RethinkCare overperformance suggests Canadian employees may prefer self-directed digital wellness tools over structured counseling. This is an important design insight: program mix (EAP vs digital tools) should be calibrated by region, not applied uniformly.

At country level, New Zealand leads APJC at 17.9% penetration (albeit on a very small base of 56 employees). UK (6.3%), Australia (6.2%), Canada (7.8%), and Costa Rica (6.4%) are the strongest country performers. India, despite being the largest APJC market (15,898 EE), is at 3.5% — below the APJC average, suggesting a potential opportunity for targeted outreach.

4.84% global reach after 7 months Canada: 7.80% (leading region) US: 5.52% (2,207 enrolled) India: 3.5% (opportunity market) +422 enrolled since August 2025
3 Burnout Risk & Mental Health Coverage Gaps
🚧 Critical Gap

Korea: Mental health insurance exclusion is a structural equity gap

Korea is the only country across Cisco’s 14 APJC markets — and likely the only country globally — where mental health is explicitly excluded from insurance coverage. 297 Korean employees have no insurance protection for mental health treatment costs. Korea also has the only missing wellbeing satisfaction score (N/A in source), which may indicate measurement gaps alongside coverage gaps.

This is not just a benefits equity issue; it creates a structural conflict with Cisco’s global Conscious Culture commitments. Mental health insurance exclusions are increasingly uncommon in the Korean market as the country has expanded coverage — Cisco should be at or above local market practice, not below it.

Korea also has 36% vacation utilization, placing it in the “high burnout risk” cluster (28-36% range with Japan and Taiwan). The combination of no mental health coverage + low vacation utilization is a particularly high-risk configuration.

Action: Escalate to APJC Benefits Director for FY27 plan renewal agenda. Obtain broker recommendation on adding mental health coverage. Target: mental health explicitly included by FY27.

Only country with NO MH coverage 297 employees affected 36% vacation utilization Wellbeing satisfaction: N/A
⚠ Burnout Cluster

Japan/Taiwan/Korea vacation utilization signals cultural burnout risk at scale

Japan (28%), Taiwan (30%), and Korea (36%) vacation utilization are the three lowest in APJC. Globally, these would rank as extreme outliers — most Cisco markets utilize 65-79% of vacation entitlement. For Japan specifically, this affects 1,453 employees and sick leave data is unavailable in the source system, meaning there is no offsetting signal to reassure that these employees are taking time to recover.

A paradox worth noting: Japan’s wellbeing satisfaction score is 9.5/10 (the highest alongside GC). This dissociation between low vacation utilization and high satisfaction is a classic pattern in high-context work cultures where employees report high satisfaction due to social desirability bias, even when objective indicators (leave utilization) suggest stress. HR should validate satisfaction data collection methodology for Japan and Korea.

For Taiwan, no wellbeing satisfaction data was available, and the country also shows 22.8% sick leave utilization — lower than might be expected in a high-stress environment, which may reflect under-reporting. Pre-existing condition exclusions in Taiwan’s insurance plan create an additional barrier if employees develop chronic health conditions from prolonged stress.

Recommended action: Design manager enablement campaigns in JP/TW/KR specifically focused on “psychological safety to take leave.” Track vacation utilization quarterly as a health span leading indicator.

Japan: 28% vacation (1,453 EE) Taiwan: 30% vacation Korea: 36% vacation Japan satisfaction 9.5/10 (paradox) India sick leave: 70.9% (different issue)
⚠ Elevated but Different

India 71% sick leave utilization: illness burden or reporting artifact?

India shows 70.9% sick leave utilization across its 15,898 employees — far above every other APJC country (most range 25-50%). India also has an insurance claim ratio of 74% (11,940 claimants / HC), which is separately verified data. These two data points together suggest genuine elevated illness burden, not purely a reporting artifact.

India also has pre-existing condition exclusions in its insurance plan and 32.2% health checkup participation (below China’s 77% and Japan’s 69% despite India being the largest APJC market). The combination — high sick leave, high claim ratio, pre-existing exclusions, below-average preventive care participation — suggests a population with real health challenges and potentially insufficient preventive infrastructure.

India’s wellbeing satisfaction of 8.9/10 is solid but the lowest of the scored markets, which is directionally consistent with the illness burden signals. EAP utilization for India is reported at 1.9% via Pulse YTD (low) but India has historically been a hard market to drive EAP utilization due to stigma. RethinkCare penetration is 3.5% (below APJC average of 4.28%), suggesting engagement with wellbeing platforms may also lag.

71% sick leave utilization 74% insurance claim ratio 32.2% health checkup (below region avg) Pre-existing exclusion in plan 8.9/10 wellbeing satisfaction 3.5% RethinkCare (below APJC avg)
4 Financial Stress as a Primary Health Driver
ℹ Cross-Region Pattern

Taxation / financial stress is the #1 EAP presenting issue in EMEA & LATAM

In both EMEA and LATAM, “Taxation” is the top presenting issue in EAP cases — ahead of anxiety, relationship issues, and other traditionally dominant mental health topics. This is a significant departure from the global (non-US) pattern where Anxious/Worry dominates. The pattern is consistent across two geographically and culturally distinct regions, which strengthens the signal considerably.

In February 2026 global Pulse data, Taxation entered the global top-3 as well, suggesting this is becoming a broader trend. The implication: for a meaningful segment of Cisco’s global workforce, financial anxiety is the primary mental health stressor — and it is being routed through the EAP rather than a financial wellness tool.

Cisco currently offers Wellthy (caregiving/family financial navigation), BrightPlan (financial wellness for US), and financial coaching components in some markets. However, these programs appear to be receiving less routing of financial stress than the EAP. This suggests either: (a) employees are unaware of financial wellness tools and default to EAP, (b) the tax/financial issues are complex enough to require EAP-level support, or (c) employees prefer the privacy of EAP for financial discussions. All three have different program design implications.

Recommendation: Work with EAP vendor to add a “warm handoff” protocol for tax/financial presenting issues to financial coaching tools. Track if this changes the presenting issue mix in subsequent quarters.

EMEA #1 issue: Taxation LATAM #1 issue: Taxation Feb 2026 global top-3: Taxation (new entry) EMEA financial coaching: BrightPlan/Wellthy underutilized
5 Preventive Health & Early Intervention
★ Strong

APJC preventive care participation is mixed but improving; Korea and Japan lead

7 of 14 APJC markets achieve ≥50% health checkup participation. Korea (100%) and Japan (69%) are the standouts, though both are driven at least partly by statutory mandates. China (77%) and Taiwan (65%) also show strong checkup participation despite not being statutory.

Vaccination participation is more varied. Japan stands out with 72.7% vaccination (likely COVID booster programs in the data period). Thailand (48.3%), Vietnam (31.1%), Korea (46.4%), and Hong Kong (16%) follow. Singapore is the only country with a “Below benchmark” grade for vaccination, suggesting a specific gap for 702 employees.

Australia and New Zealand use insurer-funded preventive care, which may explain lower direct participation numbers (6.2% and 0% respectively for checkups, despite likely higher actual participation via the insurer). The framework for measuring preventive care in these markets should be clarified to avoid understating participation.

India’s 32.2% checkup participation across 15,898 employees (the largest APJC market) represents a significant leverage point. If India’s checkup participation could be increased to China’s level (77%), approximately 7,000 additional employees would receive annual preventive care — with potential to identify and address chronic conditions earlier.

7/14 markets at ≥50% checkup Korea: 100% (statutory) Japan: 69% checkup, 72.7% vaccination India: 32.2% checkup (opportunity: +7K EE) Singapore: Below benchmark (vaccination)
★ LATAM Clinic

Mexico Onsite Clinic delivers zero-escalation primary care with strong campaign reach

The Mexico onsite clinic at two Cisco campuses served 33 employees in March 2026 with zero escalations, indicating effective triage and primary care delivery. The top diagnoses include a mix of routine (health check-up, pharyngitis) and concerning (cardiovascular: 3 cases, gastro: 8 cases) patterns.

Notably, cardiovascular disorders appearing in 3 of 33 visits (9%) in a single month is a higher proportion than might be expected for a typical primary care population in the 21-40 dominant age cohort. While n is small, this is worth tracking over 6+ months to see if it reflects a structural health trend.

The health promotion campaigns are high-leverage: vaccination education (89 attendees) and maternal & child health (79 beneficiaries) reached far more employees than the direct clinic visits, demonstrating the clinic’s value as a health communication hub beyond direct care. The gastro disorder recommendation (hand sanitizer placement at cafeterias) is an example of the clinic providing public health insights from individual case patterns.

33 visits, 0 escalations Cardiovascular: 3 of 33 (9%) — monitor Vaccination education: 89 attendees Maternal/child health: 79 beneficiaries
6 Health Investment Concentration & ROI Signal
ℹ Structural

Wellbeing & mental health is <2.5% of total health spend but delivers outsized ROI signals

FY26 plan allocation to Wellbeing & Mental Health: $19.6M. Work/Life (EAP, caregiving, family): $18.4M. Combined “health beyond physical” spend: approximately $38M. Total global health-related spend (Medical/Health + Insurance + Wellbeing + Work/Life) is approximately $875M. Wellbeing & mental health therefore represents approximately 4.3% of total health-related spend.

Against this, the EAP program alone produces a documented 23% absenteeism reduction per engaged employee. If we conservatively value a recovered absent day at 1 day’s compensation (using $49,565/year US average ≈ $191/day), the 3,240 recovered absent days from 1,800 EAP cases yield approximately $619,440 in productivity value — just from absenteeism, just in 2025, not including presenteeism improvements, turnover impact, or multi-year effects.

This back-of-envelope analysis is conservative and incomplete, but it suggests the wellbeing program cluster is producing positive return on investment. The programs most likely to have the highest ROI are those that address the highest-utilization, most-impactful issues: EAP (high utilization, direct productivity outcomes), Wellthy (caregiving reduces unplanned absence), and RethinkCare (mental resilience building at scale).

The US Onsite Health & Clinic category ($16.9M) is the only region with this spend and likely delivers significant ROI through early intervention and reduced ER utilization, but outcome data is not currently in the dashboard. Adding clinic utilization and case outcome data for the US onsite program would strengthen the ROI narrative.

Wellbeing + MH spend: $19.6M Work/Life spend: $18.4M Combined: ~4.3% of health-related spend Est. productivity value: $619K+ (EAP only, conservative) US Onsite Clinic: $16.9M (no outcome data yet)
7 Data Quality & Measurement Gaps

Key measurement gaps that limit health span analysis

  • US EAP utilization: The US is served by a separate UBH/Optum US program. US-specific EAP utilization, CSAT, and presenting issues are not in the current dashboard. With 39,963 US employees (46.7% of global HC), this is the largest data gap in health span measurement.
  • Wellthy outcome sample sizes: Q4 2025 n=29 and Q1 2026 n=53 are too small for reliable population inference. Request Wellthy to implement always-on outcome tracking vs. quarterly surveys to build statistically meaningful samples.
  • Japan sick leave data: Marked as N/A in source. With 1,453 employees and 28% vacation utilization, Japan is a high-priority burnout risk market without a key health indicator.
  • APJC mental health coverage for Korea: No utilization or satisfaction data for Korea wellbeing exists, limiting understanding of the real impact of the coverage gap.
  • Financial wellness program utilization: BrightPlan, Wellthy financial coaching, and other financial wellness tools are not tracked in the adoption dashboard, making it impossible to assess whether they are addressing the financial stress signal in EMEA/LATAM.
  • US Onsite Health & Clinic outcomes: $16.9M spend with no utilization or outcome data in the dashboard.
  • APJC EAP by sub-region: Only aggregate APJC data available; India (15,898 EE) vs Japan (1,453 EE) utilization patterns are not separately reported.
